Warm-up Question

How has information technology developed through its major stages, and how has each stage changed society?

Explanation (Part 1)

The History of Information Technology (IT)

Twenty years ago, many services we rely on today were not possible. IT has developed through a series of stages, becoming smaller and more interconnected.

Time periodMajor technologies and EventsImpact on society

1970s-80sSpread of personal computers (PCs).Beginning of personal computer use.

Moore's Law

Moore's Law is the empirical observation that 'the number of transistors on an integrated circuit doubles approximately every two years.' This led to dramatically increased computing power. However, miniaturization is approaching a physical limit, causing problems like the quantum tunneling effect and leakage current.

Explanation (Part 2)

Social Changes Resulting from IT

  • SNS (Social Networking Service): Connect users to post and share information rapidly.
  • E-commerce (EC): Buying and selling goods and services through the Internet.
  • Remote Work: Working from home or another remote location using the Internet.
  • Online Learning: Classes and materials delivered over the Internet.
  • Cashless Payment: Payment without cash (e-money, QR codes, cards).

Notable Emerging Technologies

Autonomous Driving

Uses AI to drive a vehicle without human operation. Uses edge computing for instant processing on board.

AR (Augmented Reality)

Overlays digital information on real-world images.

VR (Virtual Reality)

Allows users to immerse themselves in a virtual space generated by a computer.

Quantum Computing

Expected to dramatically speed up complex computations using qubits (superposition: 0 and 1 at once).

Lesson 2: How AI Works

HOW CONCEPT

Learning Objectives

  • Explain what AI is.
  • Understand the relationship between Machine Learning and Deep Learning.
  • Explain how generative AI is positioned within AI technologies.

Warm-up Question

What is AI, and how are machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI related?

Explanation (Part 1)

What is Artificial Intelligence (AI)?

AI is a general term for technologies that reproduce or perform intelligent human behavior: learning, reasoning, and judgment on a computer. Three everyday AI examples: speech understanding, image recognition, and translation.

[ Nested Categories Diagram: AI (Outer) -> ML -> DL -> GenAI (Inner) ]

The Relationship between AI and Machine Learning

Within AI technologies, there is machine learning; within machine learning, there is deep learning; and within deep learning, there is generative AI. Most current AI systems are narrow AI, designed to perform a specific task.

Machine Learning (ML)

One of the learning technologies that makes AI work. It learns patterns from data to make predictions, classifications, or support decisions, rather than programming every rule explicitly. Examples: spam email classification, product recommendations.

Explanation (Part 2)

Deep Learning (DL)

An advanced technology within machine learning that uses neural networks. It learns complex patterns using large-scale data. Examples: image analysis for autonomous driving, speech recognition.

Artificial Neural Network (ANN)

A system modeled after the workings of the nerve cells (neurons) of the human brain. By connecting many simple components (input layer, hidden layers, output layer), it learns from data and becomes capable of making complex judgments. While training, it updates weights to reduce error.

Generative AI (GenAI)

AI technology that uses deep learning to generate new data (text, images, audio, programs, etc.) depending on patterns learned from training data. Examples: ChatGPT, image generation AIs.

Pause & Think:
Spam filters and product recommendations are completely different tasks. What do they have in common?
Answer: Both learn patterns from data instead of being explicitly programmed with rules.

Lesson 4: Ethical Issues with AI

ETHICAL CONCEPT

Learning Objectives

  • Explain what algorithmic bias is and describe its main causes.
  • Connect the basic principles of AI ethics to specific situations.
  • Understand privacy and explainability.

Warm-up Question

What ethical issues arise as AI spreads, and what principles should guide how we use it?

Explanation (Part 1)

Algorithmic Bias

Bias in AI judgments is caused by bias in the training data. If training data is biased, an AI can reproduce that bias in its judgments. Examples include a hiring AI unfairly evaluating a particular gender, or a face-recognition AI misidentifying particular ethnic groups.

Main causes: Insufficient data on certain attributes, past discriminatory tendencies reflected in the data, or inappropriate choice of proxy variables.

Privacy Issues

The development of AI technology has given rise to new issues concerning privacy: Surveillance through face-recognition technology (cameras tracking individuals in public spaces) and mass collection of personal data online.

Explainable AI (XAI) and Responsibility

XAI is technology that makes it possible for humans to understand why an AI made a particular judgment. When an AI's process is opaque (a black box), it is difficult to verify accuracy. Responsibility deals with who answers when an AI judgment is wrong.

Explanation (Part 2)

The Fundamental Principles of AI Ethics

1. Fairness

No unjust discrimination against a person or group.

2. Transparency

Showing the AI's decision-making process and inner workings clearly.

3. Privacy Protection

Handling personal information appropriately and protecting privacy.

4. Accountability

Being answerable for the AI's decisions and outcomes.

Lesson 1: Cryptographic Technologies and Authentication

CRYPTOGRAPHIC CONCEPT

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how HTTPS secures communication.
  • Describe how the TLS handshake uses public-key and common-key cryptography.
  • Describe how multi-factor authentication is used in real services.

Warm-up Question

How do cryptographic technologies and authentication work together to keep online communication and services secure?

Explanation (Part 1)

How HTTPS Communication Works

HTTPS (HTTP Secure) is a communication protocol that adds TLS encryption to HTTP. It provides encryption, detection of tampering, and verification of the communication partner. The procedure to establish secure communication is called the TLS handshake.

StageWhat happensCryptographic method
1. Connection (Cert Verification)Server sends digital certificate & public key. Browser verifies via Certificate Authority (CA).Public-key cryptography
2. Key Exchange (Session Key)Browser creates a common key, encrypts it with the server's public key, and sends it.Public-key cryptography
3. Data TransferData is encrypted and exchanged using the shared common key.Common-key cryptography

Public-key cryptography locks with a public key and unlocks with a different private key. Common-key (symmetric-key) cryptography uses the same key for both, allowing faster data transfer.

Explanation (Part 2)

Authentication in Real Services

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) uses two different factors to verify identity. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) uses two or more factors from: Knowledge (password), Possession (smartphone/SMS), Biometrics (fingerprint). Using an independent second factor reduces the likelihood of unauthorized access if a password is compromised.

Combinations of Security Technologies

  • Encryption: Prevents communication content from being read by third parties.
  • Digital Signature: Detects data tampering or impersonation and provides non-repudiation.
  • Digital Certificate: Verifies that the communication partner is authentic.
  • Multi-factor Authentication: Prevents unauthorized logins.
Pause & Think:
If a password is leaked, why can a second factor sent to a phone still keep the account safe?
Answer: Because knowing the password alone is not enough to gain access; the attacker still needs the physical device.

Lesson 2: Network Security Design

NETWORK CONCEPT

Learning Objectives

  • Explain the roles of firewalls, VPNs, and DMZs in protecting a network.
  • Describe defense in depth.
  • Explain why layered, verified access (zero trust) is stronger than a single barrier.

Warm-up Question

How can an organization design its network so that, even if one defense is breached, the system stays protected?

Explanation (Part 1)

The Role of Firewalls and VPNs

A firewall is a system installed at the entry/exit point of a network that allows or denies communication based on predefined security rules. A VPN (Virtual Private Network) creates an encrypted virtual private line (tunnel) over the Internet, allowing safe connection to a network from a remote location. It reduces the risk of eavesdropping even on public Wi-Fi.

DMZ (Demilitarized Zone)

An area where servers exposed to the external network (web servers, mail servers) are placed separately from the internal network. Even if a public-facing server in the DMZ is attacked, damage to the internal network can be prevented.

Explanation (Part 2)

Defense in Depth

The concept of layering multiple security measures so that, even if one measure is breached, the next can still protect the system.

LayerExamples of measures
Layer 1: Network entryBlock unauthorized communication using a firewall.
Layer 2: Communication pathEncrypt communication using a VPN.
Layer 3: Server placementSeparate public-facing servers from internal network using DMZ.
Layer 4: EndpointsInstall antivirus software and keep the OS updated.

Zero Trust

Conventional network security assumed 'inside is safe, outside is dangerous' (perimeter security). With cloud services and remote work, this boundary blurred. Zero Trust is a security concept that means the system does not trust any communication (even from inside) and always verifies every access.

Lesson 3: Incident Response and Risk Management

INCIDENT CONCEPT

Learning Objectives

  • Explain what a security incident is.
  • Describe the stages of incident response in the correct order.
  • Apply the idea of risk assessment by prioritizing risks.

Warm-up Question

When a security incident happens, how should an organization respond in order — and how does it decide which risks to address first?

Explanation (Part 1)

What is a Security Incident?

A situation in which actual damage occurs due to an information security problem. Examples: Information leak (personal data disclosed), Data tampering (data altered without authorization), and Service outage (service unavailable due to attacks).

The 6 Stages of Incident Response

  • 1. Preparation: Establish response procedures in advance.
  • 2. Detection: Discover and confirm that an incident has occurred.
  • 3. Containment: Limit the scope of impact so damage does not spread further.
  • 4. Eradication: Remove the cause of the incident (e.g., remove malware).
  • 5. Recovery: Return systems and services to their normal state.
  • 6. Prevention of recurrence: Analyze the cause and document lessons learned.

Explanation (Part 2)

The Concept of Risk Assessment

Risk is the possibility of an information security problem occurring. The size of a risk is determined by: Risk size = Impact × Likelihood.

Impact: how much damage occurs if the risk materializes. Likelihood: how high the probability is. High-impact and high-likelihood risks should be addressed first.

Lesson 1: The Overall Structure of Web Applications

THE CONCEPT

Learning Objectives

  • Explain what a web application is.
  • Describe the roles of its three tiers: frontend, backend, and database.
  • Trace how the three tiers cooperate.

Warm-up Question

How is a web application structured, and how do its three tiers work together to respond to what a user does?

Explanation (Part 1)

What is a Web Application?

Web applications are applications used through a web browser. They can be used as long as you have a browser, without needing to install dedicated software (e.g., YouTube, Amazon, Gmail).

The Three Tiers of a Web Application

TierRoleExamples
FrontendThe screen component that users see directly. Runs on the browser and accepts user input.Search screen, product listing pages, input forms
BackendThe hidden server-side processing. Processes data and makes decisions.Searching for products, login authentication
DatabaseA system that organizes, stores, and manages data.Product information, user information, order history

Explanation (Part 2)

The Flow of How the Three Tiers Cooperate

When a user operates a web application, the three tiers cooperate: 1. Frontend receives input (search keywords). 2. Frontend sends keywords to Backend. 3. Backend searches the Database. 4. Database returns matching data to Backend. 5. Backend organizes results and returns to Frontend. 6. Frontend displays the results on the user's screen.

Familiar Configurations

Video sharing site (Frontend: video player; Backend: selects recommendations; Database: stores video data). E-commerce site (Frontend: cart screen; Backend: stock checking; Database: order history).

Pause & Think:
When you type in a search box and see results, which tier collected your input, and which tier found the answer?
Answer: Frontend collected the input; backend processed the request and found the answer in the database.

Lesson 2: Web Application Communication Methods

WEB CONCEPT

Learning Objectives

  • Explain the client and server model.
  • Describe how HTTP requests and responses work.
  • Explain the role of an API in exchanging data in JSON format.

Warm-up Question

How do the client and server communicate to make a web application work, and what rules and messages do they use?

Explanation (Part 1)

Client and Server

The Client is the side that requests data or processing from the server (e.g., web browsers, smartphone apps). The Server is the side that receives requests, processes them, and returns responses.

HTTP and HTTPS

HTTP (HyperText Transfer Protocol) is a protocol for exchanging data. HTTPS (HTTP Secure) adds TLS encryption, making communication content private and secure from eavesdropping.

HTTP Requests and Responses

Requests use HTTP methods: GET (retrieve data) and POST (send data for processing). Responses use Status Codes: 200 (Success), 404 (Page not found), and 500 (Server error).

Explanation (Part 2)

API and JSON

API (Application Programming Interface) is a system for exchanging data between different pieces of software. The frontend often sends requests to the backend through an API.

JSON (JavaScript Object Notation) is a data format that is easy for humans to read and for computers to process, often used in APIs.

Lesson 1: Types and Characteristics of Media

TYPES CONCEPT

Learning Objectives

Warm-up Question

What are the strengths and constraints of different media, and how do you choose the right one for a purpose and audience?

Explanation (Part 1)

Comparison of Media Characteristics

TV reaches a large audience simultaneously (one-way). Radio appeals to hearing and can be used on the go. Newspapers remain physically printed and can be read offline. Websites combine text, images, video, and audio, allowing for two-way interaction. SNS spreads information rapidly and encourages user sharing.

Strengths & Constraints of Websites

  • Strengths: Diverse information combined, updatable at any time, global access, two-way interaction (forms, comments).
  • Constraints: Normally requires an Internet connection, information doesn't reach users unless they actively seek it, variable loading speeds.

Explanation (Part 2)

Choosing Media According to Purpose

To inform many people at once -> TV, SNS ads. To provide detailed information viewable anytime -> Website. To deliver directly -> Email. To spread by word of mouth -> SNS.

In practice, multiple media are used together. For instance, a TV commercial directs viewers to a website, which encourages sharing on SNS.

Lesson 2: Information Design and User Experience

INFORMATION CONCEPT

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how personas and wireframes are used to plan a website.
  • Apply the four principles of design (CRAP).
  • Explain the idea of user-centered design.

Warm-up Question

How do designers plan a website around its users, and what principles make a page clear and easy to use?

Explanation (Part 1)

Persona and Wireframe

A Persona is a representation of a typical user built around their goals, needs, behaviors, and context. It aligns decisions with the user's perspective. A Wireframe is a rough blueprint determining the placement and priority of information without colors or decorations. Doing this first stops arguments about aesthetic preferences.

The Four Principles of Design (CRAP)

  • Contrast: Make priority clear and highlight important elements.
  • Repetition: Repeat specific characteristics for consistency.
  • Alignment: Align elements to a common baseline for visual order.
  • Proximity: Place related information close together (grouping).

Explanation (Part 2)

User-Centered Design (UCD)

UCD is an approach that designs websites from the perspective of the user, rather than the convenience of the creator. The goal is to improve usability (ease of use) and accessibility (usable by people with varied needs or impairments).

Warm-up Question

Explanation (Part 1)

Qualitative vs. Quantitative Evaluation

Qualitative evaluation explores the 'reasons' behind user behavior through observation, interviews, and usability testing. It answers 'why'. Quantitative evaluation uses numerical data to objectively measure behavior and results. It answers 'how much'.

Major Indicators in Web Analytics

IndicatorDescription
PV (Page View)Total number of times a page has been viewed.
Bounce RateProportion of visits that ended after viewing only the first page.
CVR (Conversion Rate)Proportion of visits that achieved a target action (purchase, registration).

Explanation (Part 2)

Heuristic Evaluation

A qualitative method in which experts identify UI problems based on rules of thumb (heuristics). Examples: Consistency across the site, Error prevention, and Visibility of system status (e.g., loading indicators, error messages).

Lesson 4: The Iterative Improvement Process

THE CONCEPT

Learning Objectives

  • Explain the PDCA cycle and steps of design thinking.
  • Describe how A/B testing compares two options.
  • Explain why improvement is repeated in cycles.

Warm-up Question

Why is improving a website a repeated loop rather than a one-time job, and how do teams decide what to change?

Explanation (Part 1)

The PDCA Cycle

A method that continuously advances improvement through four steps: Plan (set goals), Do (execute), Check (measure results), Act (decide next steps). Gradual improvement through repetition.

Design Thinking

  • 1. Empathize: Understand user challenges.
  • 2. Define: Clarify the problem.
  • 3. Ideate: Generate many ideas.
  • 4. Prototype: Quickly create a testable model.
  • 5. Test: Have users try it and gather feedback.

Explanation (Part 2)

A/B Testing

A method where two patterns (A and B) are shown to actual users to see which is more effective. Crucially, change only ONE element at a time, so you know what caused the difference. Make decisions only after collecting enough data.
